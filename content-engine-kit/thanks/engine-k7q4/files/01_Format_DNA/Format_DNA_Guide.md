# Format DNA: Break Down a Viral Reel and Keep What Made It Work

Format DNA is the part of a video that earned the views, separate from what the video was about. When you keep the DNA and change the message, you borrow the structure that already worked without copying anyone's content.

## The core rule
**Keep the machine. Change the message.** Keep the hook principle, the format components, the rehook, the cadence, the length, the idea type, and the reward. Change the audience wording, the angle, the examples, and the voice.

## Step 0: Pick the right reference
- Only use outliers: a reel with at least 3x its creator's median views (see 08 Creator Research). For hooks you plan to reuse word for word in structure, also require 100K+ views.
- Pick references inside formats you can actually film. If you never dance, a dance format is not your format.
- Prefer creators who used the same format more than once after it hit. A reused format is a stronger signal than a one-off.

## Step 1: Media lock (watch it, do not read it)
The caption is not the hook. The description is not the hook. Before you break anything down:
1. Open the reel and confirm it is the right one and the right creator.
2. Watch the first 10 seconds with sound on. Write the **spoken hook** word for word.
3. Write the **on-screen text** in the first 3 seconds, exactly as shown.
4. Note the total length in seconds.
If you have not heard or seen the first seconds yourself, you do not have the hook yet.

## Step 2: The 8 parts of Format DNA
| Part | What to capture | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Spoken words and on-screen text in the first 0 to 3 seconds | What tension or promise does it open? Curiosity gap, "that's me," or rule-break? |
| 2. Format components | The visual and structural building blocks | Talking head, green screen, split screen, list, story, reaction, demo, B-roll? Text style? Camera angle? |
| 3. Rehook | The line or moment that re-grabs attention mid-video | Where does it happen (seconds)? What does it promise next? |
| 4. Cadence | The rhythm of the delivery | Fast cuts or one take? Short punchy lines or long story? Pauses? |
| 5. Length | Total seconds and words | Your version should land between 0.75x and 1.25x of the reference length |
| 6. Idea | The type of idea, not the topic | Myth vs truth, mistake, list, before and after, story, hot take, how-to? |
| 7. Reward | What the viewer gets for staying to the end | A tip, a reveal, a punchline, a framework, a feeling? |
| 8. Emotional reward and next step | How the viewer should feel and what to do next | Relief, validation, motivation, curiosity? Follow, comment, save, DM? |

A quick way to estimate script length: words divided by 2.5 is roughly seconds of talking.

## Step 3: Principle plus echo (the niche shift)
This is where most people go wrong. Do not paste the reference's words into your niche. Instead:
- **Principle:** write down why the hook works in one sentence. Example: "It names a common habit and says it is costing you something."
- **Echo:** rewrite that principle in your audience's words, about your audience's world.
- **Foreign-world check:** if a noun from the original niche shows up in your version (gym words in a finance video, for example), you pasted instead of shifting. Rewrite it.

### Worked example (illustrative)
| | Reference (fitness creator) | Your version (business coach) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook principle | Names a habit the viewer does daily and says it backfires | Same |
| Original hook | "Never stretch right after you wake up" | "Never answer a lead the second they message you" |
| Format | Talking head, 3 reasons, on-screen numbered list | Same |
| Rehook | "Number 3 is the one nobody tells you" | "Number 3 is the one that costs you the most" |
| Length | 28 seconds | 25 to 33 seconds |
| Reward | A better morning routine | A better first reply that books more calls |

This example shows the method only. It is not a real reel or a result.

## Step 4: Voice lock (make it yours)
- Before writing, load your phrase bank, brand kit, and do-not-say list (03 and 05).
- Weave **at least 3 of your real lines into the middle of the script**, after the hook and before the call to action.
- Do not glue a signature line onto the end and call it your voice. The middle of the script is where your voice has to live.
- If the middle of your script still reads like the first AI draft, it has not been voice-locked yet.

## Step 5: Final DNA check before you film
- [ ] Hook principle matches the reference, in your audience's words
- [ ] Format components match
- [ ] Rehook is in roughly the same spot
- [ ] Cadence feels the same out loud
- [ ] Length is 0.75x to 1.25x the reference
- [ ] Same idea type and same kind of reward
- [ ] Emotional reward and next step are clear
- [ ] No words from the original niche
- [ ] At least 3 real phrase-bank lines in the middle
- [ ] Nothing in your brand kit's banned list
- [ ] You read it out loud and it sounds like you

## Prompt: Format DNA breakdown (paste into your AI)
```
Here is a reference reel I watched myself.
Spoken hook (first 10 seconds, word for word): [paste]
On-screen text (first 3 seconds): [paste]
Length: [seconds]
Transcript or my notes: [paste]

Break it down into the 8 parts of Format DNA: hook, format components, rehook (with timestamp), cadence, length, idea type, reward, emotional reward and next step.
Then write the hook principle in one sentence.
Do not use the caption as the hook. Mark anything you are unsure of [CONFIRM].
```
